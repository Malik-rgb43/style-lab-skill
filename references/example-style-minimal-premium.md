# Worked example: the "minimal premium" talking-head style (as taught in the source video)

This is the style the source video trains Claude on (the clean educational look of
Arthur Brooks' reels). It is here as the worked example of what a finished **style
anatomy** looks like: ten scene categories, each with its design, colour, effect and
motion rules. Use it to see the level of detail a style pack needs, or as a starting
point. It is a description of a look, not anyone's assets; build your own library from
reference videos you are allowed to study.

Source: Joseph, "How to Edit VIRAL Talking Head Videos Using ONLY Claude! (New Minimal
Style)", YouTube, 2026-10-31, chapter 1 (0:46–5:54). Facts below are what the video
says or shows on screen; where a value was read off a frame it says so.

## Why it works (the video's framing)
Special, artistic, high production, addictive to watch. The whole look comes from a small
palette, two fonts, paper/cloth textures and one accent colour used with discipline.
Every category below reuses the same textures, edges and colours; that consistency is
what makes it read as one style.

## The caption rule (applies everywhere text appears: "cinematic text")
1. Take the line about to be written (usually five words or fewer).
2. Pick the most important word or two; set them bold (bold sans, or bold serif italic) and centred.
3. Find the gaps above or under it and place the other words there, small, light sans.
4. No fixed pattern; the logic is the pattern.
Board annotation (8:59, read off the frame): "Multiple sans light small text" · "Singular or
double bold sans text" · "Distance of the light sans text is 75% of the big bold text".

## The ten categories
Board section names as they appear in the video's Figma board (10:24) are in brackets.

1. **On-screen captions** [On-Screen Text (Captions)] — A-roll with the caption rule above. Emphasis by size and font switch, not by colour boxes (a blue highlight box appears on some words).
2. **B-roll + text** [B-roll + Text + Overlay] — Split the script into small segments; find b-roll that literally shows the segment ("fighting" → a man fighting). Apply light paper texture, darken the edges, blur the edges a little so the eye goes to the focal point. Cinematic text on top describes the scene.
3. **Paintings** [Imagery / Characters + Text] — A painting that describes the point (public-domain works in the demo: Hayez "The Kiss", Chardin, Vermeer, Holbein, Watteau, Friedrich). Paint texture + rough paper texture so it feels real. Cinematic text around the painting's main subject. Reveal with a quick camera slide-in.
4. **Line + text** [Line + Text Animations] — Black or white paper ground. A simple accent-blue line leads up to the text, is interrupted by it, then continues into related text. The camera tracks where the line goes so the viewer follows exactly what is being said.
5. **Line + illustrations** [Line + Illustrations] — Same ground and line, but the line leads into illustrations. Variants: (a) horizontal line, white dot marks the object, cinematic text describes it, illustration below; (b) line starts from text, draws a zigzag, camera pans to reveal objects on both sides — used to show progression through phases; (c) the line leads straight up to the scene's subject.
6. **Text-centric scenes** [Text only scenes] — White or blue ground with very rough paper texture; two quotation marks around the centre; cinematic text in the middle writes out the line. Simple and to the point.
7. **Center illustrations** [Center Illustration + Text] — One illustration of the script's main idea (e.g. a person) on white paper, in a rough, painted, noisy texture, filling the lower half and bleeding into the upper middle, with abstract edges. The rest of the frame holds related cinematic text. Variants: top-down brain image; circular pattern of thin lines with clean text; side profiles of characters in rough cloth texture to contrast against the ground.
8. **Side illustration + text** [Side Illustration + Text] — Same ground and abstract characters, on one side of the frame, cinematic text on the other so the frame feels complete. Variant: rough-cloth half-circle on the left edge with clean text rotating around it. Used to show two elements at once.
9. **Object + text** (rare) [Objects / Elements + Text] — A single element as part of the story; paper ground; cinematic text around it; rough reveal animations.
10. **Graphs + statistics** (rare) [Graphs + Visual Representations] — Flat dark textured ground; grid of thin white glowing lines; a blue ball is the line's head and the camera follows it as it moves.

## Scene types used by the plan document
Every beat in the edit plan is one of three types (the legend under the beat timeline, 15:41):
- **A-roll + captions** — stay on the speaker, captions only (category 1).
- **Overlay on A-roll** — a graphic or word over the speaker (e.g. a box around a word, a timeline over A-roll).
- **Cut-away scene** — leave the speaker for a full-frame scene (categories 2–10).
